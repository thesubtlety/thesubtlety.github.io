I’ve worked in security since 2012, primarily in red teaming, adversarial engineering, and cyber defense.

I was part of the founding red team at Capital One, conducted adversary simulations at Symantec, led a red team at NortonLifeLock, and worked as a Staff Adversarial Engineer at Okta. My work has included adversary emulation, threat research, detection engineering, and building tools to test security controls.

In 2023, I co-founded [Adversis](https://www.adversis.io/), a small security consulting practice that has served more than 50 organizations. Our work spans penetration testing, risk assessments, and security and privacy engineering. I work with business leaders, product teams, and engineers to identify security priorities and put them into practice.

I also co-founded [Puck Security](https://puck.security/), an early-stage venture exploring credential security and post-compromise access. We built and operated the software in a small production environment.

My technical interests include credential recovery, post-exploitation activity, agentic security, and the effectiveness of security controls.

I co-authored [_The Effective Red Team: Adversary Emulation Inside the Enterprise_](https://nostarch.com/effective-red-team) (No Starch Press, 2027) with Trevin Edgeworth and Jordan Potti.

Most of my programming background is in Go and Python. My open-source projects include [tailsnitch](https://github.com/adversis/tailsnitch), [go-decrypt-jenkins](https://github.com/thesubtlety/go-decrypt-jenkins), and [agent-scrub](https://github.com/thesubtlety/agent-scrub). I’ve also contributed to [Sliver](https://github.com/BishopFox/sliver), [Metasploit](https://github.com/rapid7/metasploit-framework), and [Hashcat](https://github.com/hashcat/hashcat). More of my work is on the [tools](/tools/) and [research](/post/) pages.
